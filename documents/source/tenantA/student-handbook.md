# Chapter 1. The University

## 1.1 History

Bagwis University was founded on **September 18, 1968** by the educator Dr. Aurelio M. Santillan, who believed that quality higher education should be within reach of every hardworking young Filipino. It began as the Bagwis Institute, a small school of commerce and teacher education with 212 students and nine teachers, holding classes in a two-story building along Kalayaan Avenue in Lungsod ng Sampaguita.

The institute added engineering in 1975 and nursing in 1983. In 1991 it was granted university status and became Bagwis University. Today it has five colleges and serves students from many provinces. Every year on September 18 the university celebrates **Foundation Day**, and there are no classes on that day.

## 1.2 Vision

Bagwis University envisions itself as a leading learner-centered university that forms competent, compassionate and globally aware Filipino professionals.

## 1.3 Mission

Bagwis University provides accessible, high-quality education through excellent teaching, relevant research and active community service. It forms graduates who think critically, act ethically and lead with service.

# Chapter 4. Admission and Enrollment

## 4.1 Admission

New students apply through the Office of Admissions, Agila Hall Room 110 (Director: Ms. Kristine Joy M. Bautista). Applicants take the Bagwis University College Admission Test (BUCAT), which costs ₱500. Applicants who pass receive a Notice of Admission, also called the admission slip.

## 4.2 Enrollment in Brief

Continuing students enroll in eight steps:

1. Clear your balances so that your BagwisNet clearance says "Cleared".
2. Check your grades and curriculum evaluation on BagwisNet.
3. Meet your academic adviser and get a signed advising slip.
4. Select your courses on BagwisNet.
5. Get your assessment of fees from the Accounting Office.
6. Pay at the Cashier or online (full payment or the ₱5,000 down payment).
7. Get validated by the Registrar and download your Certificate of Registration (COR).
8. Get your ID validation sticker at the Office of Student Affairs.

You are officially enrolled once your COR is issued. Freshmen bring their admission slip, SHS report card (Form 138), PSA birth certificate, Certificate of Good Moral Character, two 2x2 ID photos and a medical clearance from the University Clinic.

# Chapter 5. Academic Policies

## 5.1 Grading System

Bagwis University uses a scale where **higher is better**:

| Grade | Percentage | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 4.0 | 96–100 | Excellent (highest) |
| 3.5 | 91–95 | Superior |
| 3.0 | 86–90 | Very Good |
| 2.5 | 81–85 | Good |
| 2.0 | 76–80 | Satisfactory |
| 1.5 | 71–75 | Fair |
| 1.0 | 65–70 | Passed (lowest passing grade) |
| 0.0 | Below 65 | Failed |

The final grade is 40% of the midterm grade plus 60% of the final term grade. The General Weighted Average (GWA) is weighted by units, excludes NSTP, and is truncated to two decimal places (never rounded up).

## 5.2 Incomplete Grades

An INC must be completed within one semester, meaning by the last day of the final examinations of the next regular semester. If it is not completed, it automatically becomes **0.0**. To complete an INC, get a completion form from the Registrar and pay ₱150 per course at the Cashier.

## 5.3 Attendance

- Absences above **20%** of class hours may result in a grade of FA (failure due to absences), which counts as 0.0.
- More than 15 minutes late = late. More than 30 minutes late = absent.
- Three lates equal one absence.

## 5.4 Academic Standing

| Standing | Main trigger | Main effect |
|---|---|---|
| Academic Warning | Failed units 25% to less than 50%, or semester GWA below 2.00 | Maximum 18 units |
| Academic Probation | Failed units 50% to less than 75%, or a second consecutive warning | Maximum 15 units; all university scholarships and grants suspended; not eligible for Latin honors |
| Dismissal from program | Failed units 75% or more, probation not lifted, or same course failed three times | May apply once to shift to another program |

## 5.5 Honors

- **Dean's List:** semester GWA of 3.50 or higher, at least 15 units, no grade below 2.5, and no INC, W, 0.0 or FA.
- **President's List:** same conditions with a GWA of 3.75 or higher.
- **Latin honors:** Summa cum laude 3.80 and above; Magna cum laude 3.60 to 3.79; Cum laude 3.40 to 3.59; plus no grade below 2.0 and never on probation.

# Chapter 8. Campus Safety and Emergencies

## 8.1 Campus Security

The Campus Security Office is at Gate 1 and is open **24 hours a day**. The Chief of Security is Mr. Dante R. Velasco. Security guards check IDs at every gate, patrol the campus, and respond to emergencies. In any emergency on campus, call Campus Security at **local 199** or approach the nearest guard.

## 8.2 Emergency Alerts

Official emergency announcements are sent by text message to the mobile number registered on BagwisNet, and are also posted on BagwisNet and the official Bagwis University page. Students must keep their mobile number updated. Fake alerts sometimes spread through group chats, especially during typhoon season. To help students tell real alerts from fake ones, every official text alert from the Campus Security Office ends with the verification code BLUE-HERON-42, and any alert without this code should be checked with the Security Office before it is shared.

## 8.3 Class Suspension Due to Weather

All classes are **automatically suspended** when:

- Tropical Cyclone Wind Signal No. 2 or higher is raised over Lungsod ng Sampaguita; or
- an orange or red rainfall warning is issued for the area.

For other situations, the President decides on suspensions. Announcements are posted on BagwisNet and the official Bagwis University page by **5:00 AM** for morning classes and by **11:00 AM** for afternoon classes. When classes are suspended, offices may remain open unless the announcement says otherwise. Missed examinations are rescheduled by the Registrar.

## 8.4 Earthquake Drills and Evacuation

The university holds an earthquake and fire drill at least once every semester. During an earthquake, students should "duck, cover and hold", then follow the marked evacuation routes to the open grounds in front of the Bagwis Gymnasium once the shaking stops. Do not use elevators during an earthquake or fire. Class marshals take the attendance of their class at the evacuation area.

## 8.5 Fire Safety

Fire extinguishers are placed on every floor of every building. Students who see smoke or fire should leave the building, warn others, and call Campus Security. Cooking appliances are not allowed in classrooms or organization offices.

## 8.6 Medical Emergencies

The University Clinic responds to medical emergencies on campus from 7:00 AM to 7:00 PM, Monday to Saturday. Outside those hours, Campus Security arranges transport to the nearest hospital and informs the student's emergency contact.

## 8.7 Lost and Found

Lost items are surrendered to the Lost and Found at the Security Office, Gate 1. Items are kept for **30 days**. Unclaimed items are then donated. To claim an item, describe it and show your student ID.

# Chapter 9. Student Conduct and Discipline

## 9.1 General Principle

Every Bagwisian is expected to act with respect, honesty and responsibility, on campus and online, and during off-campus activities that represent the university.

## 9.2 Student ID

- The student ID must be worn **at all times** on campus.
- A student who forgets the ID may get a temporary gate pass from Campus Security, but only **3 times per semester**.
- A lost ID is replaced at the OSA for ₱300 with an affidavit of loss.
- The ID must have the validation sticker for the current semester.

## 9.3 Dress Code

- The university uniform is worn **Monday to Thursday**.
- **Friday is "wash day"**: students may wear decent casual clothes.
- Not allowed at any time: slippers, shorts, sleeveless tops and ripped jeans.
- Laboratory and clinical courses may require special uniforms, as announced by the college.

## 9.4 Minor Offenses

Examples of minor offenses include not wearing the ID, violating the dress code, littering, eating inside the library or laboratories, using a mobile phone during class without permission, and making unnecessary noise in hallways during classes.

| Offense | Sanction |
|---|---|
| First offense | Verbal warning |
| Second offense | Written reprimand |
| Third offense | Treated as a major offense; 20 hours of community service |

## 9.5 Major Offenses

Examples of major offenses include cheating, plagiarism, fighting, vandalism, hazing, forging or altering documents, hacking university systems, bringing weapons, and possessing or using illegal drugs. Sanctions range from suspension of **3 to 15 school days**, to exclusion (being dropped from the rolls), to expulsion.

## 9.6 Academic Dishonesty

| Offense | Sanction |
|---|---|
| First offense | 0.0 on the exam or requirement |
| Second offense | 0.0 in the course and 5 school days of suspension |
| Third offense | Dismissal from the university |

## 9.7 Due Process

No student is sanctioned for a major offense without due process:

1. The student receives a written notice of the complaint.
2. The student has **5 working days** to submit a written answer.
3. The Student Discipline Board holds a hearing, where the student may present evidence and be accompanied by a parent or adviser.
4. The Board issues a written decision.
5. The student may appeal to the President within 5 working days after receiving the decision.

## 9.8 Effect on Honors and Scholarships

A major offense removes eligibility for Latin honors and leads to the loss of any scholarship. A disciplinary sanction in a semester removes eligibility for the Dean's List for that semester.

# Chapter 10. Student Organizations and Activities

The Bagwis Supreme Student Council (BSSC) is the highest student government body. Its office is on the 2nd floor of Maya Hall. Officers are elected every March. Students on Academic Probation may not run for the BSSC.

## 10.2 Accreditation of Organizations

Every student organization must be accredited by the OSA **every semester**. The deadlines are **July 31, 2026** for the first semester and **December 18, 2026** for the second semester. To be accredited, an organization needs:

- at least **15 members** who are enrolled students;
- a faculty adviser;
- a constitution and by-laws;
- a list of officers; and
- a plan of activities for the semester.

Unaccredited organizations may not use the university name, reserve rooms, or hold activities on campus.

## 10.3 Holding Activities

Activities must be approved by the OSA at least **10 working days** before the event. Off-campus activities also need a parent's consent form for every participant and an accompanying faculty adviser. Activities may not be held during examination weeks.

## 10.4 Anti-Hazing

Hazing in any form is strictly prohibited, in line with Philippine law. Initiation rites must be non-violent and approved by the OSA in advance. Any member who takes part in hazing faces expulsion.

# Chapter 11. Student Rights and Responsibilities

## 11.1 Student Rights

Every Bagwisian has the right to:

- receive quality instruction and know the course requirements and grading system at the start of each course;
- be treated fairly and with respect, free from discrimination and harassment;
- form and join accredited organizations;
- express opinions freely and responsibly;
- due process in disciplinary cases;
- the privacy of personal information and records; and
- appeal grades and decisions through proper channels.

## 11.2 Student Responsibilities

Every Bagwisian has the responsibility to:

- attend classes regularly and on time;
- follow university rules and the laws of the land;
- respect the rights of others;
- take care of university property; and
- keep personal information on BagwisNet, including emergency contacts and mobile number, up to date.

## 11.3 Grievances

A student with a complaint about a faculty member, staff member or office should first try to settle it directly and respectfully. If not settled, the student may file a written complaint with the dean (for academic matters) or with the OSA (for non-academic matters). Complaints are acted on within 10 working days. Complaints of sexual harassment go directly to the Committee on Decorum and Investigation through the OSA, and are handled confidentially.

# Chapter 13. Technology and Online Conduct

## 13.1 Acceptable Use

University computers, Wi-Fi and BagwisNet accounts are for academic and official use. Students must not:

- share their password or use someone else's account;
- try to access systems or data without permission;
- download or share pirated software, films or music;
- send spam, threats or harassing messages; or
- install software on laboratory computers without permission.

Violations are major offenses when they involve hacking or harassment.

## 13.2 Social Media

Students should remember that what they post online can affect others and the university. Posting insulting, false or private content about classmates, teachers or staff may be treated as a disciplinary offense. Only the Office of the President and the OSA may post on behalf of the university.

## 13.3 Use of AI Tools

Instructors decide whether AI writing or coding tools may be used in their courses, and must state the rule in the course syllabus. Using AI tools when the instructor has not allowed it, or presenting AI-generated work as your own without saying so, is academic dishonesty.

## 15.1 Students with Disabilities

Bagwis University welcomes students with disabilities. A student who needs accommodations, such as extra time in examinations, a seat near the front, accessible classrooms, or materials in larger print, should register with the Office of Student Affairs at the start of each semester and present a medical or professional certificate. The OSA then sends a confidential accommodation letter to the student's instructors. Students do not need to explain their condition to each instructor.

## 15.2 Mental Health and Well-being

The Guidance and Counseling Center runs well-being activities every semester, including stress-management workshops before midterm and final examinations, peer support circles, and a "Kumustahan" week in September. Students who feel overwhelmed are encouraged to talk to a counselor early. Seeking help is a sign of strength and never affects a student's grades or records.

## 15.3 Pregnant Students and Student Parents

Pregnant students may continue their studies and may not be dropped or penalized because of pregnancy. They may request reasonable adjustments in physical activities, laboratory work and attendance through the OSA, supported by a doctor's certificate. Student parents may ask the OSA about schedule adjustments when a child is sick.

## 15.4 Smoke-Free and Vape-Free Campus

Smoking and vaping are not allowed anywhere on campus, including parking areas and inside vehicles parked on campus. Violations are treated as minor offenses under Chapter 9.

## 15.5 Gender and Respect

Every student has the right to be addressed respectfully. Bullying, discrimination and harassment based on gender, religion, ethnicity, disability, economic status or appearance are not tolerated and are handled as disciplinary offenses. Complaints of sexual harassment go to the Committee on Decorum and Investigation through the OSA and are handled confidentially.

## 15.6 Food and Nutrition

The Kapitbahay Food Court in Maya Hall offers affordable meals, including at least one vegetable dish and one meal under ₱80 every day. Drinking water stations are found on every floor of Agila Hall, Kalapati Hall and the Lawin Building. Students are encouraged to bring reusable water bottles.

## 15.7 Physical Activity

The Bagwis Gymnasium is open to enrolled students for free recreational use when it is not reserved for classes or varsity training, usually Monday to Friday, 4:00 PM to 7:00 PM. Students must present their validated ID and wear proper sports attire.

# Chapter 16. Parking, Transport and Facilities Use

## 16.1 Parking

Students who bring vehicles must get a parking sticker from the Campus Security Office at Gate 1. The sticker costs **₱500 per semester for a car** and **₱250 per semester for a motorcycle**. Applicants show their validated student ID, a valid driver's license, and the vehicle's registration. Parking is first come, first served, and the university is not responsible for loss or damage to parked vehicles.

## 16.2 Parking Rules

- Park only in marked student parking areas. Faculty and visitor slots are reserved.
- The speed limit inside the campus is 15 kilometers per hour.
- Vehicles may not stay on campus after 10:00 PM without a permit from the OSA.
- Vehicles without a valid sticker may be refused entry.

## 16.3 Bicycles

Bicycle racks are available beside Gate 1 and behind the Lawin Building. Bicycles do not need a sticker, but students should use their own locks. Bicycles may not be brought inside buildings.

## 16.4 Getting to Campus

Jeepneys and tricycles along Kalayaan Avenue stop in front of Gate 1. During heavy rain, students are encouraged to leave early and to check BagwisNet for announcements about class suspensions.

## 16.5 Reserving Rooms and Facilities

Accredited organizations may reserve classrooms, the audio-visual room and the Bagwis Gymnasium for approved activities. Reservations are made at the OSA at least **10 working days** before the event, together with the activity approval. The organization is responsible for cleaning up and for any damage to the facility.

## 16.6 Lockers

A limited number of lockers are available on the ground floor of the Lawin Building. Lockers are assigned per semester by the OSA on a first come, first served basis, for a fee of ₱200 per semester. Lockers must be emptied by the last day of final examinations. Items left behind are surrendered to the Lost and Found.

# Chapter 17. Data Privacy

## 17.1 How We Use Your Information

Bagwis University collects personal information from students to manage admission, enrollment, grades, scholarships, health services, discipline and communication. The university follows the **Data Privacy Act of 2012**. Personal information is shared outside the university only when required by law, or with the student's written consent (for example, when a TOR is sent to an employer at the student's request).

## 17.2 Your Rights

Students have the right to be informed about how their data is used, to access their data, to ask for corrections, and to file a complaint. Requests are sent to the Data Protection Officer, **Atty. Noel B. Garcia**, Agila Hall 2nd floor, dpo@bagwisu.example, local 115.

## 17.3 Grades and Parents

Grades are released only to the student through BagwisNet. Parents or guardians may receive grade information only if the student signs a consent form at the Registrar, or if the student is a minor.
