# 1. Purpose, Scope and Definitions

## 1.1 Purpose

This Enrollment and Registration Policy explains how students of Bagwis University enroll each semester, what documents they must submit, which offices they must visit, and which deadlines apply. It is issued by the Office of the University Registrar (OUR) under the authority of the Office of the Vice President for Academic Affairs (OVPAA). The policy aims to make enrollment predictable, fair and fast for every Bagwisian, whether entering the university for the first time or continuing in a program.

This document is the official reference for enrollment dates and procedures for Academic Year (AY) 2026–2027. Where another university document gives a different enrollment date, this policy prevails, and the Registrar will correct the other document.

## 1.2 Scope

This policy applies to all undergraduate students of all five colleges of Bagwis University:

- College of Arts and Sciences (CAS)
- College of Business and Accountancy (CBA)
- College of Engineering and Computing (CEC)
- College of Education (CED)
- College of Nursing and Health Sciences (CNHS)

It covers the first semester, the second semester and the midyear (summer) term. Graduate programs follow a separate graduate school calendar, but they use the same offices and the same fees for documents.

## 1.3 Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Academic Year (AY) | The school year, made up of a first semester, a second semester and a midyear term. AY 2026–2027 starts with the first semester in June 2026. |

| Regular enrollment | The main enrollment period for a term. |
| Late enrollment | A short period after regular enrollment, with a late enrollment fee of ₱500. |
| Clearance | Confirmation from the Library, Accounting Office and Office of Student Affairs that a student has no unpaid balance, unreturned book or pending requirement. |

# 3. Who May Enroll

## 3.1 New Students (Freshmen)

A freshman may enroll only after receiving a **Notice of Admission** (also called the admission slip) from the Office of Admissions. To be admitted, an applicant must have completed Senior High School (SHS) and passed the Bagwis University College Admission Test (BUCAT). The BUCAT fee is ₱500 and is paid at the Cashier before the test.

Some programs have additional admission requirements. For example, the College of Nursing and Health Sciences requires an SHS general average of at least 85, and the College of Engineering and Computing requires a passing score in the mathematics part of the BUCAT. Applicants are told about these requirements by the Office of Admissions.

## 3.2 Continuing Students

A continuing student may enroll if the student:

- was enrolled in the previous regular semester;
- is not dismissed from the program or debarred from the university under the Grading and Academic Policy;
- has clearance from the Library, the Accounting Office and the Office of Student Affairs; and
- has no pending disciplinary sanction that bars enrollment.

Students on Academic Warning or Academic Probation may still enroll, but with a reduced maximum load (see Section 6).

## 3.3 Transferees

Students coming from another college or university may apply for admission as transferees through the Office of Admissions. Transferees must also take the BUCAT unless they have completed at least 36 units at their previous school with a general average equivalent to 2.5 or better on the Bagwis scale.

## 3.4 Returning Students

A student who stopped studying for one or more semesters **without** an approved Leave of Absence is a returning student. Returning students must file a readmission form at the Registrar, secure clearance, and meet with the dean of their college. The dean may require the student to follow the current curriculum instead of the curriculum in force when the student first entered.

## 3.5 Students on Leave of Absence

A student who had an approved Leave of Absence (LOA) may enroll again without a readmission form, as long as the leave did not go beyond the approved period. An LOA may last at most two consecutive semesters. A student who does not return after the LOA ends becomes a returning student.

# 4. Enrollment Requirements

## 4.1 Requirements for Freshmen

Freshmen submit the following documents to the Registrar, Agila Hall Room 101:

1. Notice of Admission (admission slip) from the Office of Admissions.
2. Original Senior High School report card (Form 138), showing completion of Grade 12.
3. Photocopy of the PSA birth certificate. The original must be shown for checking and is returned immediately.
4. Certificate of Good Moral Character from the last school attended.
5. Two (2) recent 2x2 ID photos with white background and name tag.
6. Medical clearance from the University Clinic, Maya Hall ground floor.

The medical clearance is given after a basic physical examination at the University Clinic. The examination is free and is done on a walk-in basis from Monday to Saturday, 7:00 AM to 7:00 PM. Students should get the medical clearance before the last day of regular enrollment.

## 4.2 Requirements for Transferees

Transferees submit:

1. Notice of Admission from the Office of Admissions.
2. Honorable Dismissal (also called transfer credentials) from the previous school.
3. Copy of the Transcript of Records (TOR) for evaluation purposes.
4. Certificate of Good Moral Character from the previous school.
5. Photocopy of the PSA birth certificate (original shown for checking).
6. Two (2) recent 2x2 ID photos.
7. Medical clearance from the University Clinic.

## 4.3 Credit Evaluation for Transferees

The dean of the receiving college evaluates the courses taken at the previous school. Only courses with a grade equivalent to **2.0 or better** on the Bagwis grading scale can be credited. At most **50%** of the total units of the Bagwis program may be credited. The evaluation is done within ten working days after all documents are complete. A transferee whose evaluation is not yet finished may enroll provisionally in general education courses only.

## 4.4 Requirements for Continuing Students

Continuing students do not need to submit documents again. They need:

- a clearance status of "Cleared" on BagwisNet (no unpaid balance, no unreturned library books, no pending OSA requirement);
- a signed advising slip from their academic adviser; and
- their previous semester's grades, viewable on BagwisNet.

## 4.5 Requirements for Returning Students

Returning students need a readmission form (from the Registrar), clearance, and the dean's approval. If the student attended another school while away, the student must also submit a TOR from that school.

## 4.6 Incomplete Documents

A freshman or transferee who lacks one document may be allowed to enroll with a signed promissory letter to submit the document within 30 days after the first day of classes. A student who does not submit the document by then will not be able to download grades or request any certificate until the document is submitted. The Notice of Admission and the medical clearance can never be deferred.

# 5. Step-by-Step Enrollment Procedure

## 5.1 Steps for Continuing Students

Continuing students follow these eight steps:

1. **Clear your balances.** Make sure your BagwisNet clearance status says "Cleared". If not, settle unpaid fees at the Accounting Office, return library books to Banog Library, and finish any pending OSA requirement.
2. **Check your grades and curriculum evaluation.** Log in to BagwisNet and open "My Grades" and "Curriculum Evaluation". This shows which courses you have passed and which you still need.
3. **Meet your academic adviser.** Bring your curriculum evaluation. Your adviser will approve the courses you may take and sign your advising slip.
4. **Select your courses online.** On BagwisNet, open "Enrollment", then "Course Selection", and choose your sections. Upload a photo of your signed advising slip.
5. **Get your assessment of fees.** The Accounting Office reviews your selected courses and posts your assessed fees on BagwisNet, usually within one working day.
6. **Pay.** Pay either the full amount or the down payment of ₱5,000 at the Cashier (Agila Hall Room 103) or through the online payment options listed on BagwisNet.
7. **Get validated by the Registrar.** After your payment is posted, the Registrar validates your enrollment and issues your Certificate of Registration (COR). You can download the COR from BagwisNet.
8. **Validate your ID.** Bring your COR and your student ID to the Office of Student Affairs, Kalapati Hall Room 201, to get the ID validation sticker for the semester.

You are officially enrolled after Step 7, when the COR is issued. Step 8 is required to enter the campus after the second week of classes.

## 5.2 Steps for Freshmen and Transferees

1. Get your Notice of Admission from the Office of Admissions, Agila Hall Room 110.
2. Get your medical clearance from the University Clinic.
3. Submit all requirements in Section 4 to the Registrar, Agila Hall Room 101. The Registrar creates your student record and gives you your student number (format YYYY-NNNNN) and your BagwisNet account.
4. Attend the freshman advising session of your college, where you receive your block schedule.
5. Get your assessment of fees from the Accounting Office.
6. Pay the full amount or the ₱5,000 down payment at the Cashier.
7. Get your COR from the Registrar.
8. Have your ID photo taken at the Office of Student Affairs. The ID is released within five working days.

## 5.3 Steps for Returning Students

Returning students first file the readmission form and get the dean's approval, then follow the same steps as continuing students starting at Step 1.

## 5.4 Where to Ask for Help

The Help Desk of the Office of Student Affairs answers general enrollment questions at helpdesk@bagwisu.example or local 200. Questions about records and the COR go to the Registrar. Questions about fees go to the Accounting Office. Problems logging in to BagwisNet go to the ICT Services Office, Lawin Building Room 105.

# 6. Unit Load

## 6.1 Regular Load

The regular load is the number of units listed in the student's curriculum for that semester, usually 18 to 24 units. The maximum load in a regular semester is **24 units**.

## 6.2 Overload

A student may take up to **27 units** in a regular semester only if all of these are true:

- the student is graduating at the end of that semester or the following midyear term;
- the student's GWA in the previous semester was **3.00 or higher**; and
- the dean of the college approves the overload in writing.

## 6.3 Reduced Load because of Academic Standing

| Academic standing | Maximum load |
|---|---|
| Good standing | 24 units (27 with approved overload) |
| Academic Warning | 18 units |
| Academic Probation | 15 units |
| Midyear term (any standing) | 9 units |

Academic standing is decided under the Grading and Academic Policy. Students who receive a scholarship should also check the Scholarships and Financial Aid Policy, because some scholarships require a minimum load (for example, the Bagwis Academic Excellence Scholarship requires at least 18 units).

## 6.4 Underload

A student may take fewer units than the regular load. Underloading may delay graduation, and students with scholarships may lose them if they go below the scholarship's minimum load. Students are advised to talk to their adviser before underloading.

# 7. Changes After Enrollment

## 7.1 Adding and Dropping Courses

After enrolling, a student may add or drop courses until the add/drop deadline:

- First semester: {{ADDDROP_1ST}}
- Second semester: December 4, 2026

To add or drop, the student files a change-of-course form at the Registrar with the adviser's signature. Each transaction costs ₱100. A dropped course does not appear on the transcript. After the add/drop deadline, a student who wants to stop attending a course must withdraw from it instead (Section 7.2).

## 7.2 Official Withdrawal from a Course (W)

A student may withdraw from a course after the add/drop deadline and up to the withdrawal deadline:

- First semester: August 28, 2026
- Second semester: February 12, 2027

The course stays on the transcript with a grade of **W**, which does not count in the GWA. After the withdrawal deadline, no W is given. A student who stops attending after the deadline will receive the grade earned, which is often 0.0 or FA (failure due to absences).

## 7.3 Fees after Dropping or Withdrawing

Refunds depend on when the student drops or withdraws:

| When the student drops or withdraws | Refund of tuition and laboratory fees |
|---|---|
| Before classes begin | 100% of all fees, minus a ₱1,000 processing fee |
| Within the first week of classes | 75% of tuition and laboratory fees (miscellaneous fee not refundable) |
| Within the second week of classes | 50% of tuition and laboratory fees |
| After the second week of classes | No refund; all fees remain payable |

Amounts paid by a scholarship or grant are never refunded to the student. They go back to the scholarship fund.

## 7.4 Shifting to Another Program

A student may shift to another program only during a regular enrollment period. Requirements:

- cumulative GWA of **2.50 or higher**;
- written approval from the dean of the current college and the dean of the new college; and
- a shifting fee of ₱200, paid at the Cashier.

A student may shift at most **two times** while at Bagwis University. A student who was dismissed from a program may apply once to shift to another program under the Grading and Academic Policy.

## 7.5 Cross-Enrollment

A student may take up to **6 units** per semester at another school only if the course is not offered at Bagwis that semester and the student is graduating within the year. A cross-enrollment permit from the Registrar is required before enrolling at the other school. Students on Academic Probation may not cross-enroll.

## 7.6 Leave of Absence

A student who must stop studying for a time may file a Leave of Absence (LOA) at the Registrar, with the dean's approval. The LOA must be filed by the withdrawal deadline of the semester (August 28, 2026 for the first semester and February 12, 2027 for the second semester). An LOA can last at most two consecutive semesters. Fees follow the refund schedule in Section 7.3.

# 8. Fees and Payment at Enrollment

## 8.1 Fees for AY 2026–2027

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| Tuition | ₱1,450 per unit |
| Laboratory fee | ₱1,800 per laboratory course |
| Miscellaneous fee | ₱6,500 per regular semester; ₱2,000 for the midyear term |
| Late enrollment fee | ₱500 |
| Change of course (add/drop), per transaction | ₱100 |
| Shifting fee | ₱200 |
| BUCAT (entrance test) | ₱500 |

Example: a student taking 21 units with one laboratory course pays ₱30,450 tuition (21 × ₱1,450), ₱1,800 laboratory fee, and ₱6,500 miscellaneous fee, for a total of ₱38,750 before any discount or scholarship.

## 8.2 Payment Options

Students choose one of two payment plans when they pay:

- **Full payment.** Pay everything at enrollment. Students who pay in full by the last day of regular enrollment get a **5% discount on tuition** (not on laboratory or miscellaneous fees).
- **Installment plan.** Pay a down payment of ₱5,000 at enrollment plus an installment plan fee of ₱750. The remaining balance is divided into three equal installments.

| Installment | First semester | Second semester |
|---|---|---|
| 1st installment | July 24, 2026 | December 11, 2026 |
| 2nd installment | August 14, 2026 | January 15, 2027 |
| 3rd installment | October 9, 2026 | March 5, 2027 |

Each late installment has a ₱200 penalty. Students with an overdue balance cannot take the midterm or final examinations unless the Accounting Office approves a promissory note. Unpaid balances block enrollment for the next term.

## 8.3 Students with Scholarships

Scholarships and grants are deducted during the assessment of fees (Step 5). A student whose scholarship result is still pending may enroll by paying the down payment. Any excess is credited to the student's account after the scholarship is approved. See the Scholarships and Financial Aid Policy for details.

## 9.1 The Certificate of Registration

The COR is the official proof of enrollment. It lists the student's name, student number, program, year level, courses, sections, schedules, units and assessed fees. Students should check their COR carefully. Errors must be reported to the Registrar within the add/drop period.

## 9.2 Certificates and Records

| Document | Fee | Processing time |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate of Enrollment | ₱100 | 1 working day |
| Certificate of Grades | ₱100 | 2 working days |
| Transcript of Records (TOR) | ₱150 per page | 5 to 7 working days |
| Honorable Dismissal | ₱300 | 5 working days |

A representative may claim documents with an authorization letter and the IDs of both the student and the representative. The Certificate of Good Moral Character is issued by the Office of Student Affairs, not the Registrar.

## 9.3 Data Privacy

Enrollment information is personal information protected under the Data Privacy Act of 2012. The Registrar uses it only for academic records and enrollment. Questions about personal data go to the Data Protection Officer, Atty. Noel B. Garcia, at dpo@bagwisu.example.

# 10. Frequently Asked Questions on Enrollment

### When is enrollment for the first semester?

Regular enrollment for the first semester of AY 2026–2027 is from {{ENROLL_1ST}}. Late enrollment, with a ₱500 fee, is from {{LATE_1ST}}. Classes begin on June 29, 2026.

### When is enrollment for the second semester?

Regular enrollment is from November 3 to 14, 2026. Late enrollment is from November 16 to 20, 2026. Classes begin on November 23, 2026.

### Can I enroll if I still have a balance from last semester?

No. Your BagwisNet clearance must say "Cleared". Pay your balance at the Cashier first, or talk to the Accounting Office about a payment arrangement.

### What if I miss both regular and late enrollment?

You cannot enroll for that semester. You may file a Leave of Absence so that you do not become a returning student, or wait for the next term.

### Is the late enrollment fee refundable?

No. The ₱500 late enrollment fee is not refundable, even if you later withdraw.

### Can my parent enroll for me?

Steps 1 to 6 can be done online or by a representative. The representative needs an authorization letter and the IDs of both the student and the representative. Freshmen must appear in person for the ID photo.

### How many units can I take?

The maximum is 24 units in a regular semester, or 27 units with an approved overload for graduating students. Students on Academic Warning can take at most 18 units, and students on Academic Probation at most 15 units. The midyear term maximum is 9 units.
